Ex 3.1 • Q1(i) 3-Variable System
Solve the following system of linear equations in three variables:
$x + y + z = 5$;   $2x - y + z = 9$;   $x - 2y + 3z = 16$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.1 • Q1(i) Elimination Method
Must-Use Formula / Concept Eliminate one variable (say $y$) from two pairs of equations to get a $2$-variable system in $x$ and $z$, solve for $x, z$, and back-substitute.
Step 1 (Label Equations)
Let:
$x + y + z = 5 \quad \cdots (1)$
$2x - y + z = 9 \quad \cdots (2)$
$x - 2y + 3z = 16 \quad \cdots (3)$
Step 2 (Eliminate y from (1) & (2))
Adding $(1)$ and $(2)$:
$(x + y + z) + (2x - y + z) = 5 + 9 \implies 3x + 2z = 14 \quad \cdots (4)$
Step 3 (Eliminate y from (1) & (3))
Multiply $(1)$ by $2$ and add to $(3)$:
$2x + 2y + 2z = 10$
$x - 2y + 3z = 16$
Adding gives: $3x + 5z = 26 \quad \cdots (5)$
Step 4 (Solve for z, x, y)
Subtract $(4)$ from $(5)$:
$(3x + 5z) - (3x + 2z) = 26 - 14 \implies 3z = 12 \implies \mathbf{z = 4}$.
Substitute $z = 4$ in $(4)$: $3x + 2(4) = 14 \implies 3x = 6 \implies \mathbf{x = 2}$.
Substitute $x = 2, z = 4$ in $(1)$: $2 + y + 4 = 5 \implies \mathbf{y = -1}$.
Solution: $\mathbf{x = 2,\ y = -1,\ z = 4}$.
Unique Solution: (2, -1, 4)
Ex 3.1 • Q1(ii) 3-Variable System
Solve the following system of linear equations in three variables:
$\dfrac{1}{x} - \dfrac{2}{y} + 4 = 0$;   $\dfrac{1}{y} - \dfrac{1}{z} + 1 = 0$;   $\dfrac{2}{z} + \dfrac{3}{x} = 14$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.1 • Q1(ii) Reciprocal Substitution
Must-Use Formula / Concept Let $a = \dfrac{1}{x},\ b = \dfrac{1}{y},\ c = \dfrac{1}{z}$ to convert the reciprocal equations into a standard linear system.
Step 1 (Substitute Reciprocals)
Put $a = \dfrac{1}{x},\ b = \dfrac{1}{y},\ c = \dfrac{1}{z}$:
$a - 2b = -4 \quad \cdots (1)$
$b - c = -1 \quad \cdots (2)$
$3a + 2c = 14 \quad \cdots (3)$
Step 2 (Eliminate b)
Multiply $(2)$ by $2$: $2b - 2c = -2 \quad \cdots (4)$.
Add $(1)$ and $(4)$: $(a - 2b) + (2b - 2c) = -4 - 2 \implies a - 2c = -6 \quad \cdots (5)$.
Step 3 (Solve for a, c, b)
Add $(3)$ and $(5)$:
$(3a + 2c) + (a - 2c) = 14 - 6 \implies 4a = 8 \implies \mathbf{a = 2}$.
From $(5)$: $2 - 2c = -6 \implies 2c = 8 \implies \mathbf{c = 4}$.
From $(2)$: $b - 4 = -1 \implies \mathbf{b = 3}$.
Step 4 (Invert to find x, y, z)
$x = \dfrac{1}{a} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{2}}, \quad y = \dfrac{1}{b} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{3}}, \quad z = \dfrac{1}{c} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{4}}$.
Solution: $\mathbf{x = \dfrac{1}{2},\ y = \dfrac{1}{3},\ z = \dfrac{1}{4}}$.

Ex 3.1 • Q1(iii) 3-Variable System
Solve the following system of equations:
$x + 20 = \dfrac{3y}{2} + 10 = 2z + 5 = 110 - (y + z)$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.1 • Q1(iii) Chained Equality System
Must-Use Formula / Concept Equate adjacent pairs from the chain $A = B = C = D$ to form three linear equations in $x, y, z$.
Step 1 (Form Linear Equations)
From first & second parts: $x + 20 = \dfrac{3y}{2} + 10 \implies 2x + 40 = 3y + 20 \implies 2x - 3y = -20 \quad \cdots (1)$.
From second & third parts: $\dfrac{3y}{2} + 10 = 2z + 5 \implies 3y + 20 = 4z + 10 \implies 3y - 4z = -10 \quad \cdots (2)$.
From third & fourth parts: $2z + 5 = 110 - y - z \implies y + 3z = 105 \quad \cdots (3)$.
Step 2 (Solve (2) & (3) for y, z)
Multiply $(3)$ by $3$: $3y + 9z = 315 \quad \cdots (4)$.
Subtract $(2)$ from $(4)$: $(3y + 9z) - (3y - 4z) = 315 - (-10) \implies 13z = 325 \implies \mathbf{z = 25}$.
Substitute $z = 25$ in $(3)$: $y + 3(25) = 105 \implies y = 105 - 75 \implies \mathbf{y = 30}$.
Step 3 (Find x from (1))
Substitute $y = 30$ in $(1)$:
$2x - 3(30) = -20 \implies 2x - 90 = -20 \implies 2x = 70 \implies \mathbf{x = 35}$.
Step 4 (Verification)
Check all parts: $x+20 = 55$, $\frac{3(30)}{2}+10 = 55$, $2(25)+5 = 55$, $110-(30+25) = 55$. All equal $55$!
Solution: $\mathbf{x = 35,\ y = 30,\ z = 25}$.
Each part equals 55
Ex 3.1 • Q2 Nature of Solutions (i)–(iii)
Discuss the nature of solutions of the following system of equations:
(i) $x + 2y - z = 6$;   $-3x - 2y + 5z = -12$;   $x - 2z = 3$
(ii) $2y + z = 3(-x + 1)$;   $-x + 3y - z = -4$;   $3x + 2y + z = -\dfrac{1}{2}$
(iii) $\dfrac{y + z}{4} = \dfrac{z + x}{3} = \dfrac{x + y}{2}$;   $x + y + z = 27$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.1 • Q2 Identity vs Contradiction vs Unique
Must-Use Formula / Concept • $0 = 0$ (Identity) $\implies$ Infinitely many solutions.
• $0 = k\ (k \neq 0)$ (Contradiction) $\implies$ No solution.
• Unique values for $(x, y, z) \implies$ Unique solution.
Part (i)
Given: $x + 2y - z = 6 \cdots (1)$ and $-3x - 2y + 5z = -12 \cdots (2)$.
Adding $(1)$ and $(2)$ eliminates $y$: $-2x + 4z = -6 \implies x - 2z = 3 \cdots (4)$.
Subtracting $(3)\ x - 2z = 3$ from $(4)$ gives $\mathbf{0 = 0}$ (an identity).
$\therefore$ The system has infinitely many solutions.
Part (ii)
Rewrite $(1)$: $3x + 2y + z = 3 \cdots (1)$.
Equation $(3)$ is $3x + 2y + z = -\dfrac{1}{2} \cdots (3)$.
Subtracting $(3)$ from $(1)$ gives $\mathbf{0 = \dfrac{7}{2}}$, which is a contradiction (absurd equation).
$\therefore$ The system is inconsistent and has no solution.
Part (iii)
Let $\dfrac{y+z}{4} = \dfrac{z+x}{3} = \dfrac{x+y}{2} = k \implies y+z = 4k,\ z+x = 3k,\ x+y = 2k$.
Adding all three: $2(x+y+z) = 9k$. Since $x+y+z = 27$, $2(27) = 9k \implies k = 6$.
Thus $y+z = 24,\ z+x = 18,\ x+y = 12$. Subtracting each from $x+y+z = 27$ gives $\mathbf{x = 3,\ y = 9,\ z = 15}$.
$\therefore$ The system has a unique solution $(x = 3, y = 9, z = 15)$.
(i) Infinitely many solutions  |  (ii) No solution  |  (iii) Unique solution $(x = 3,\ y = 9,\ z = 15)$.

Ex 3.1 • Q3 Age Word Problem
Vani, her father and her grand father have an average age of $53$. One-half of her grand father's age plus one-third of her father's age plus one-fourth of Vani's age is $65$. Four years ago if Vani's grandfather was four times as old as Vani then how old are they all now?
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.1 • Q3 3-Variable Age Problem
Must-Use Formula / Concept Let the present ages of Vani, her father, and her grandfather be $x, y, z$ years respectively.
Step 1 (Form Equations)
• Average age is $53$: $\dfrac{x + y + z}{3} = 53 \implies x + y + z = 159 \quad \cdots (1)$
• Second condition: $\dfrac{z}{2} + \dfrac{y}{3} + \dfrac{x}{4} = 65$. Multiplying by $\text{LCM}(2,3,4) = 12$:
$3x + 4y + 6z = 780 \quad \cdots (2)$
• Four years ago: $z - 4 = 4(x - 4) \implies 4x - z = 12 \quad \cdots (3)$
Step 2 (Eliminate y)
Multiply $(1)$ by $4$: $4x + 4y + 4z = 636 \quad \cdots (4)$.
Subtract $(4)$ from $(2)$: $(3x + 4y + 6z) - (4x + 4y + 4z) = 780 - 636 \implies -x + 2z = 144 \quad \cdots (5)$.
Step 3 (Solve for x, z, y)
Multiply $(3)$ by $2$: $8x - 2z = 24 \quad \cdots (6)$.
Add $(5)$ and $(6)$: $7x = 168 \implies \mathbf{x = 24}$.
From $(3)$: $z = 4(24) - 12 = 96 - 12 \implies \mathbf{z = 84}$.
From $(1)$: $24 + y + 84 = 159 \implies \mathbf{y = 51}$.
Present Ages: Vani $= 24$ years, Father $= 51$ years, Grandfather $= 84$ years.

Ex 3.1 • Q4 3-Digit Number Problem
The sum of the digits of a three-digit number is $11$. If the digits are reversed, the new number is $46$ more than five times the former number. If the hundreds digit plus twice the tens digit is equal to the units digit, then find the original three-digit number?
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.1 • Q4 Place-Value Representation
Must-Use Formula / Concept Let the three-digit number be $100x + 10y + z$ (hundreds digit $= x$, tens digit $= y$, units digit $= z$). Reversed number $= 100z + 10y + x$.
Step 1 (Form Equations)
• Sum of digits: $x + y + z = 11 \quad \cdots (1)$
• Reversed number condition: $100z + 10y + x = 5(100x + 10y + z) + 46$
$\implies 499x + 40y - 95z = -46 \quad \cdots (2)$
• Digit relation: $x + 2y = z \implies x + 2y - z = 0 \quad \cdots (3)$
Step 2 (Find y and Express x, z)
Add $(1)$ and $(3)$: $2x + 3y = 11 \implies x = \dfrac{11 - 3y}{2}$.
From $(1)$: $z = 11 - x - y$.
Step 3 (Solve for x, y, z)
Multiply $(1)$ by $95$: $95x + 95y + 95z = 1045$. Add to $(2)$:
$594x + 135y = 999 \implies 22x + 5y = 37 \quad \cdots (4)$ (dividing by $27$).
From $2x + 3y = 11$, multiply by $11$: $22x + 33y = 121 \quad \cdots (5)$.
Subtract $(4)$ from $(5)$: $28y = 84 \implies \mathbf{y = 3}$.
Then $2x + 3(3) = 11 \implies \mathbf{x = 1}$, and $z = 1 + 2(3) \implies \mathbf{z = 7}$.
The original three-digit number is $100(1) + 10(3) + 7 = \mathbf{137}$.

There are $12$ pieces of five, ten and twenty rupee currencies whose total value is ₹$105$. When first $2$ sorts are interchanged in their numbers its value will be increased by ₹$20$. Find the number of currencies in each sort.
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.1 • Q5 Currency Denomination System

• Total number of notes: $x + y + z = 12 \quad \cdots (1)$

• Interchanging $x$ and $y$ increases value by ₹$20$:
$10x + 5y + 20z = 105 + 20 = 125 \implies 2x + y + 4z = 25 \quad \cdots (3)$

Subtract $(2)$ from $(3)$: $x - y = 4 \quad \cdots (4)$.

Ex 3.2 • Q1(i) & (ii) GCD by Long Division
Find the GCD of the given polynomials:
(i) $x^4 + 3x^3 - x - 3$,   $x^3 + x^2 - 5x + 3$
(ii) $x^4 - 1$,   $x^3 - 11x^2 + x - 11$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.2 • Q1(i) & (ii) Euclidean Polynomial Division
Must-Use Formula / Concept Divide $f(x)$ by $g(x)$ until the remainder is $0$. The last non-zero divisor (made monic) is the GCD.
Part (i) Step 1
Let $f(x) = x^4 + 3x^3 + 0x^2 - x - 3$ and $g(x) = x^3 + x^2 - 5x + 3$.
Dividing $f(x)$ by $g(x)$ gives quotient $q_1(x) = x + 2$ and remainder:
$r_1(x) = 3x^2 + 6x - 9 = 3(x^2 + 2x - 3)$.
Part (i) Step 2
Now divide $g(x) = x^3 + x^2 - 5x + 3$ by $(x^2 + 2x - 3)$:
$x^3 + x^2 - 5x + 3 = (x^2 + 2x - 3)(x - 1) + 0$.
Since remainder $= 0$, $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{x^2 + 2x - 3}$ (or $(x + 3)(x - 1)$).
Part (ii) Step 1
Let $f(x) = x^4 - 1$ and $g(x) = x^3 - 11x^2 + x - 11$.
Dividing $f(x)$ by $g(x)$ with quotient $x + 11$ leaves remainder:
$r_1(x) = 120x^2 + 120 = 120(x^2 + 1)$.
Part (ii) Step 2
Divide $g(x) = x^3 - 11x^2 + x - 11$ by $(x^2 + 1)$:
$x^3 - 11x^2 + x - 11 = (x^2 + 1)(x - 11) + 0$.
Since remainder $= 0$, $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{x^2 + 1}$.
(i) $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{x^2 + 2x - 3}$  |  (ii) $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{x^2 + 1}$.

Ex 3.2 • Q1(iii) & (iv) GCD by Long Division
Find the GCD of the given polynomials:
(iii) $3x^4 + 6x^3 - 12x^2 - 24x$,   $4x^4 + 14x^3 + 8x^2 - 8x$
(iv) $3x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 3$,   $6x^3 + 12x^2 + 6x + 12$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.2 • Q1(iii) & (iv) Common Factor + Long Division
Must-Use Formula / Concept Always factor out common numerical and monomial factors ($x$, constants) first, find their GCD, and multiply by the GCD of the remaining polynomials.
Part (iii) Step 1
Factor out common monomials:
$f(x) = 3x(x^3 + 2x^2 - 4x - 8)$ and $g(x) = 2x(2x^3 + 7x^2 + 4x - 4)$.
Common factor of $3x$ and $2x$ is $\mathbf{x}$.
Part (iii) Step 2
Divide $2x^3 + 7x^2 + 4x - 4$ by $x^3 + 2x^2 - 4x - 8$ (quotient $= 2$):
Remainder $r_1(x) = 3x^2 + 12x + 12 = 3(x^2 + 4x + 4)$.
Divide $x^3 + 2x^2 - 4x - 8$ by $x^2 + 4x + 4$ (quotient $= x - 2$):
Remainder $r_2(x) = 4x + 8 = 4(x + 2)$.
Since $x^2 + 4x + 4 = (x + 2)^2$, remainder is $0$. Multiplying by $x$ gives $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{x(x + 2)}$.
Part (iv) Step 1
Factor out numerical constants:
$f(x) = 3(x^3 + x^2 + x + 1)$ and $g(x) = 6(x^3 + 2x^2 + x + 2)$.
$\text{GCD}(3, 6) = \mathbf{3}$.
Part (iv) Step 2
Subtract $(x^3 + x^2 + x + 1)$ from $(x^3 + 2x^2 + x + 2)$:
Remainder $= x^2 + 1$.
Now $(x^3 + x^2 + x + 1) = (x^2 + 1)(x + 1) + 0$.
Multiplying by $\text{GCD}(3, 6) = 3$ gives $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{3(x^2 + 1)}$.
(iii) $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{x(x + 2)}$  |  (iv) $\text{GCD} = \mathbf{3(x^2 + 1)}$.

Ex 3.2 • Q2(i)–(vi) LCM of Algebraic Expressions
Find the LCM of the given expressions:
(i) $4x^2 y,\ 8x^3 y^2$    (ii) $-9a^3 b^2,\ 12a^2 b^2 c$    (iii) $16m,\ -12m^2 n^2,\ 8n^2$
(iv) $p^2 - 3p + 2,\ p^2 - 4$    (v) $2x^2 - 5x - 3,\ 4x^2 - 36$
(vi) $(2x^2 - 3xy)^2,\ (4x - 6y)^3,\ 8x^3 - 27y^3$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.2 • Q2(i)–(vi) Prime Factorization & Highest Power
Must-Use Formula / Concept $\text{LCM}$ is the product of the LCM of numerical coefficients and the highest power of each distinct algebraic factor.
(i), (ii) & (iii)
• (i) $4x^2 y,\ 8x^3 y^2$: $\text{LCM}(4, 8) = 8$; highest powers $x^3, y^2 \implies \mathbf{8x^3 y^2}$.
• (ii) $-9a^3 b^2,\ 12a^2 b^2 c$: $\text{LCM}(-9, 12) = -36$; highest powers $a^3, b^2, c \implies \mathbf{-36a^3 b^2 c}$.
• (iii) $16m,\ -12m^2 n^2,\ 8n^2$: $\text{LCM}(16, -12, 8) = -48$; highest powers $m^2, n^2 \implies \mathbf{-48m^2 n^2}$.
(iv) & (v)
• (iv) $p^2 - 3p + 2 = (p - 1)(p - 2)$ and $p^2 - 4 = (p - 2)(p + 2)$.
$\implies \text{LCM} = \mathbf{(p - 1)(p - 2)(p + 2)}$.
• (v) $2x^2 - 5x - 3 = (2x + 1)(x - 3)$ and $4x^2 - 36 = 4(x^2 - 9) = 4(x - 3)(x + 3)$.
$\implies \text{LCM} = \mathbf{4(2x + 1)(x - 3)(x + 3)}$.
(vi)
Factor each expression:
• $(2x^2 - 3xy)^2 = [x(2x - 3y)]^2 = x^2(2x - 3y)^2$
• $(4x - 6y)^3 = [2(2x - 3y)]^3 = 8(2x - 3y)^3$
• $8x^3 - 27y^3 = (2x)^3 - (3y)^3 = (2x - 3y)(4x^2 + 6xy + 9y^2)$
Taking highest powers: $\text{LCM} = \mathbf{8x^2(2x - 3y)^3(4x^2 + 6xy + 9y^2)}$.
(i) $8x^3 y^2$  |  (ii) $-36a^3 b^2 c$  |  (iii) $-48m^2 n^2$  |  (iv) $(p-1)(p-2)(p+2)$  |  (v) $4(2x+1)(x-3)(x+3)$  |  (vi) $8x^2(2x-3y)^3(4x^2+6xy+9y^2)$.
LCM = Highest Power of Each Factor

Ex 3.3 • Q1(i)–(iii) Verify f(x)×g(x) = LCM×GCD
Find the LCM and GCD for the following and verify that $f(x) \times g(x) = \text{LCM} \times \text{GCD}$:
(i) $21x^2 y,\ 35xy^2$    (ii) $(x^3 - 1)(x + 1),\ (x^3 + 1)$    (iii) $(x^2 y + xy^2),\ (x^2 + xy)$
✍️ STEP-BY-STEP SOLUTION — Ex 3.3 • Q1(i)–(iii) Product Identity Verification
Must-Use Formula / Concept For any two polynomials $f(x)$ and $g(x)$: $f(x) \times g(x) = \text{LCM}[f(x), g(x)] \times \text{GCD}[f(x), g(x)]$.
Part (i)
$f(x) = 21x^2 y = 3 \times 7 x^2 y$,   $g(x) = 35xy^2 = 5 \times 7 xy^2$.
• $\text{GCD} = 7xy$ and $\text{LCM} = 105x^2 y^2$.
• $f(x) \times g(x) = 21x^2 y \times 35xy^2 = 735x^3 y^3$.
• $\text{LCM} \times \text{GCD} = 105x^2 y^2 \times 7xy = 735x^3 y^3$. Hence verified!
Part (ii)
$f(x) = (x^3 - 1)(x + 1) = (x - 1)(x^2 + x + 1)(x + 1)$,   $g(x) = x^3 + 1 = (x + 1)(x^2 - x + 1)$.
• $\text{GCD} = (x + 1)$ and $\text{LCM} = (x^3 - 1)(x^3 + 1)$.
• $f(x) \times g(x) = (x^3 - 1)(x + 1)(x^3 + 1) = \text{LCM} \times \text{GCD}$. Hence verified!
Part (iii)
$f(x) = x^2 y + xy^2 = xy(x + y)$,   $g(x) = x^2 + xy = x(x + y)$.
• $\text{GCD} = x(x + y)$ and $\text{LCM} = xy(x + y)$.
• $f(x) \times g(x) = x^2 y(x + y)^2 = [xy(x + y)] \times [x(x + y)] = \text{LCM} \times \text{GCD}$. Hence verified!
(i) $\text{GCD}=7xy,\ \text{LCM}=105x^2y^2$  |  (ii) $\text{GCD}=x+1,\ \text{LCM}=(x^3-1)(x^3+1)$  |  (iii) $\text{GCD}=x(x+y),\ \text{LCM}=xy(x+y)$. Verified in all cases!
