Unit Ex 1 - Q 1 If the ordered pairs $(x^2 - 3x, y^2 + 4y)$ and $(-2, 5)$ are equal, then find $x$ and $y$.
✍️ SOLUTION — Unit Ex 1 - Q 1 Equality of Ordered Pairs
Must-Use Formula Two ordered pairs $(a, b) = (c, d) \iff a = c$ and $b = d$.
Step 1
Equate First Coordinates to Find $x$:
$x^2 - 3x = -2 \implies x^2 - 3x + 2 = 0$
$\implies (x - 1)(x - 2) = 0 \implies x = 1, 2$.
Step 2
Equate Second Coordinates to Find $y$:
$y^2 + 4y = 5 \implies y^2 + 4y - 5 = 0$
$\implies (y + 5)(y - 1) = 0 \implies y = -5, 1$.
Answer: $x = 1, 2$ and $y = -5, 1$.
Ordered Pair Equality: $x \in \{1, 2\}$, $y \in \{-5, 1\}$

Unit Ex 1 - Q 6 If $f(x) = x^2$, $g(x) = 3x$ and $h(x) = x - 2$, Prove that $(f \circ g) \circ h = f \circ (g \circ h)$.
✍️ SOLUTION — Unit Ex 1 - Q 6 Associativity of Function Composition
Must-Use Formula Compute LHS $= ((f \circ g) \circ h)(x)$ and RHS $= (f \circ (g \circ h))(x)$ and show LHS $=$ RHS.
Step 1
Find LHS $= (f \circ g) \circ h$:
$(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x)) = f(3x) = (3x)^2 = 9x^2$.
$((f \circ g) \circ h)(x) = (f \circ g)(h(x)) = (f \circ g)(x - 2) = 9(x - 2)^2 \cdots (1)$.
Step 2
Find RHS $= f \circ (g \circ h)$:
$(g \circ h)(x) = g(h(x)) = g(x - 2) = 3(x - 2)$.
$(f \circ (g \circ h))(x) = f(3(x - 2)) = [3(x - 2)]^2 = 9(x - 2)^2 \cdots (2)$.
Step 3
Compare $(1)$ and $(2)$:
LHS $=$ RHS $= 9(x - 2)^2 = 9(x^2 - 4x + 4) = 9x^2 - 36x + 36$.
Hence Proved: $(f \circ g) \circ h = f \circ (g \circ h) = 9(x - 2)^2$.
Composition Pipeline: $x \xrightarrow{h} x-2 \xrightarrow{g} 3(x-2) \xrightarrow{f} 9(x-2)^2$

Substitute $f(x)$ Inside $f$:

Must-Use Formula $n^2 - n = n(n - 1)$ is the product of two consecutive integers; every integer is of the form $2q$ or $2q + 1$.
Step 1
Factorize $n^2 - n$:
$n^2 - n = n(n - 1)$, which is the product of two consecutive integers.
Step 2
Case 1 ($n$ is even, $n = 2q$):
$n^2 - n = 2q(2q - 1)$, which is clearly divisible by $2$.
Step 3
Case 2 ($n$ is odd, $n = 2q + 1$):
$n^2 - n = (2q + 1)(2q + 1 - 1) = 2q(2q + 1)$, which is also divisible by $2$.
Hence Proved: $n^2 - n$ is divisible by $2$ for every positive integer $n$.
Parity Analysis: $n(n-1)$ is Always Even
