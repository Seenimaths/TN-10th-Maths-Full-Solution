Ex 2.1 - Q 1 Find all positive integers, when divided by $3$ leaves remainder $2$.
✍️ SOLUTION — Exercise 2.1 Question 1 Euclid's Division Lemma
Must-Use Formula Euclid's Division Lemma: $a = bq + r$, where $0 \le r < b$
Step 1
Let $a$ be any positive integer. Here divisor $b = 3$ and remainder $r = 2$.
Step 2
By Euclid's Division Lemma:
$a = 3q + 2$, where $q$ is a non-negative integer ($q = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$).
Step 3
Substitute $q = 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$:
When $q = 0 \implies a = 3(0) + 2 = 2$
When $q = 1 \implies a = 3(1) + 2 = 5$
When $q = 2 \implies a = 3(2) + 2 = 8$
When $q = 3 \implies a = 3(3) + 2 = 11$, and so on.
Answer: The required positive integers are $2, 5, 8, 11, 14, \dots$ (of the form $3q + 2$).
Number Line Jumps of $+3$ Starting at Remainder $r = 2$

Ex 2.1 - Q 3 Prove that the product of two consecutive positive integers is divisible by $2$.
✍️ SOLUTION — Exercise 2.1 Question 3 Even & Odd Case Proof
Must-Use Concept Let consecutive integers be $n - 1$ and $n$ (or $n$ and $n + 1$). Every integer is of the form $2q$ (even) or $2q + 1$ (odd).
Step 1
Let $n - 1$ and $n$ be two consecutive positive integers. Their product is $n(n - 1)$.
Case (i)
When $n$ is even ($n = 2q$):
$n(n - 1) = 2q(2q - 1)$, which is clearly a multiple of $2$ (divisible by $2$).
Case (ii)
When $n$ is odd ($n = 2q + 1$):
$n(n - 1) = (2q + 1)(2q + 1 - 1) = 2q(2q + 1)$, which is also divisible by $2$.
Hence Proved: The product of any two consecutive positive integers is always divisible by $2$.
Parity Tree: $n(n-1)$ is Always Even ($2k$)

Ex 2.1 - Q 8 If $d$ is the Highest Common Factor of $32$ and $60$, find $x$ and $y$ satisfying $d = 32x + 60y$.
✍️ SOLUTION — Exercise 2.1 Question 8 Linear Combination (Bézout's Identity)
Must-Use Formula Find $d = \text{HCF}(32, 60)$ by Euclid's Algorithm, then back-substitute remainders to get $d = 32x + 60y$.
Step 1
Apply Euclid's Division Algorithm to $60$ and $32$:
$60 = 32 \times 1 + 28 \quad \cdots (1)$
$32 = 28 \times 1 + 4 \quad \cdots (2)$
$28 = 4 \times 7 + 0$. Thus, $d = \text{HCF}(32, 60) = 4$.
Step 2
Reverse Substitution from $(2)$ and $(1)$:
From $(2)$: $4 = 32 - 28 \times 1$
Substitute $28 = 60 - 32 \times 1$ from $(1)$:
$4 = 32 - (60 - 32)$ $= 32 + 32 - 60$ $= 32(2) + 60(-1)$.
Step 3
Comparing $4 = 32(2) + 60(-1)$ with $d = 32x + 60y$, we get $x = 2$ and $y = -1$.
Answer: $x = 2$ and $y = -1$ (with $d = 4$).
Back-Substitution: $4 = 32(2) + 60(-1)$
